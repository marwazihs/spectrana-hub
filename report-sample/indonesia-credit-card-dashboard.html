Indonesia credit card story

Indonesia’s credit card market kept expanding nationally — but regional momentum split sharply between Jakarta and Bali.

The national market shows durable long-term growth in cards, transaction volume, and spending value. But beneath that steady national story, the regional picture diverges: DKI Jakarta intensified usage while Bali’s transaction activity weakened materially.

The story in one glance

Three ideas explain most of what matters in this dataset.

1. National growth stayed intact. Indonesia added more cards over time, but transaction volume and spending value grew even faster than card count.
2. Usage intensity is doing the heavy lifting. Recent growth is being driven less by new card issuance and more by existing cardholders using cards more often and for higher-value spend.
3. Regional outcomes are not uniform. Jakarta looks like a maturing, high-throughput market, while Bali shows a weaker transaction recovery despite an expanding card base.

Why this matters

If you were building a strategy on top of this market, the national story says “growth is real,” but the regional story says “deployment needs to be selective.”

For market sizing — national transaction value is still compounding meaningfully.
For go-to-market — high-usage metros may outperform card-growth regions.
For timing — December matters disproportionately, while February is consistently softer.

National market trend

Cards grew steadily, but transaction volume and transaction value accelerated more strongly — especially after the pandemic recovery.
